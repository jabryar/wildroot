# Wildroot Village

Open `index.html` in a web browser to play. No terminal, server, account, or installation is required.

## Environmental learning

All original survival, ecosystem, building, weather, population, storage and forestry rules remain active. The learning layer explains those rules without making the game easier or changing resources:

- The live **Eco Coach** combines staffed daytime effects with automatic overnight effects into a weighted 24-hour ecosystem rate, then focuses on the weakest or fastest-falling indicator. It identifies the strongest modelled pressure and recovery source, explains the indicator’s wider system connections, and no longer reverses advice merely because night begins.
- The **Environmental Field Guide** includes a live six-indicator evidence snapshot and explains forests, biodiversity, water and nutrient cycles, living soil, food webs, ecosystem services, emissions and noise pollution prevention, renewable yield, carrying capacity, clean technology, ecological thresholds, habitat networks, circular materials, One Health, nature-based solutions, seasonal resilience and intergenerational planning.
- Twenty field missions ask the player to demonstrate each principle through ordinary gameplay. Every lesson now includes a real-world connection.
- Twenty-two optional knowledge checks explain both correct and incorrect answers. They pause time and have no resource reward or penalty.
- A model-literacy note explains that the six scores are simplified indicators and that real decisions also need field evidence, local and Indigenous knowledge, uncertainty and impacts beyond the map.
- Every building inspection includes an environmental lesson and a decision question. Random events include a learning lens, and each scenario briefing names the environmental idea it is designed to teach.

Learning and knowledge-check progress save with the current village.

## Controls

- Explore a 100 × 100 forest world with an irregular buildable clearing.
- Building plans are ordered by the number of citizens required and then alphabetically. Choose one, then click enough free clearing tiles for its footprint.
- Drag the map to pan. Use the mouse wheel or the map buttons to zoom.
- Press **O** to rotate the selected building left and **P** to rotate it right.
- Use **Inspect** to examine a building and **Destroy** to dismantle one and recover 50% of its original materials.
- With **Inspect** active, hold a standing tree for 0.65 seconds to toggle its logging priority. Holding an already marked tree removes the priority.
- Cursor descriptions appear 20 px above or below the pointer. Use the **Descriptions on/off** button in the map’s upper-left corner to hide or restore them; the choice is saved with the village.
- With **Inspect** active, hover and click a moving resident to see their persistent name, role, workplace, destination, and carried items.
- Use **Pause**, **1×**, **2×**, and **3×** at the top. One day lasts three minutes at 1×.
- Press **Space** to pause or resume and **Esc** to cancel the current tool.

Staffed buildings operate from 07:00 until 18:30 in spring and autumn, 20:30 in summer, and 17:30 in winter. Their output, staffed services, logging work, operational pollution and workplace noise stop while residents sleep. Buildings with no jobs operate automatically through the night; consumption, weather, aging and natural tree growth also continue, so overnight storage matters. Resource and Eco Coach `xx/day` trends—and the noise costs shown in inspections—use weighted 24-hour averages rather than switching between daytime and nighttime rates.

Freeplay begins with ten adult founders. Villagers move at the faster walking pace between homes, jobs, parks, the Children’s Green, and school. They stay on clearing land, can use the open path space around artwork inside a building footprint, and never walk through the building’s central icon. Every resident receives a persistent housing assignment, enters that house at night, remains hidden indoors, and emerges after 07:00. Inspecting housing reports how many assigned residents are currently inside. Workers have a 1–3 item carrying capacity and make supply trips between their assigned workplace and storage. The population includes children, adults, and elders: a resident is a child until age 7, an adult until their individual 35–40 day elder threshold, then an elder for the rest of life. Village Schools educate up to 16 children each and improve long-term productivity and environmental practices. Each resident receives a hidden deterministic natural death age from 40–60 days. Settlers welcomed from travelling families remain for 20–40 days after arrival while still receiving a valid total death age in that range. A resident inspection shows only the villager’s current age, never their hidden lifespan, remaining time, or predicted final day. The 2× and 3× controls accelerate age, childhood growth, days, seasons, weather and all other simulation time by exactly the selected multiplier.

Workplaces and individual citizens can each be assigned **Low**, **Normal**, or **High** work priority from their inspection panel. Required job slots are always assigned before optional helper positions; within each tier, High workplaces fill before Normal and Low workplaces. Eligible High-priority citizens are considered first for those slots, followed by Normal and Low citizens, with adults ahead of elders when priorities match. A child’s chosen priority is saved and takes effect after adulthood. Priorities only control automatic assignment order—they do not change a person’s health, capability, carrying capacity, or inherent worth.

Every saved 100 × 100 world now contains two winding, one-tile forest creeks and a winding, three-tile-wide river. Rivers and creeks visibly freeze for the whole winter, including sunny and cloudy winter weather, then thaw when spring begins. Winter ice remains blocked to ordinary walking and construction because natural ice can hide flowing water and uneven thickness; bridges remain the safe crossings. A 1 × 3 Creek Footbridge spans one creek tile, while a 1 × 5 River Bridge spans three river tiles; rotate the matching bridge so its first and last tiles rest on cleared opposite banks. Every deck tile is walkable, and the water remains visible and connected beneath it. This models why a bridge generally preserves flow and aquatic movement better than filling a channel with solid ground.

With a full two-person crew, Logging Camps now fell an outside-zone tree in five base in-game hours. They remain 10× faster inside the circular 15 × 15 work zone, giving a 30-minute base time there. Weather, education, illness and incomplete staffing can change the exact duration. A stump follows exactly the same location-based rate as a tree. Each staffed camp chooses its nearest marked tree or stump; when several camps choose the same marked target outside their circles, they work together. Each logger contributes 0.5× the standard two-worker rate: four workers finish a five-hour outside job in 2.5 hours, while six finish it in about 1 hour 40 minutes. Every healthy wild tree and managed Wood Farm tree has a stable 5–10 timber yield, shown when relevant; permanent fire-damaged trees are black and leafless and supply exactly 30% less. Timber is stored only when that visible tree is actually felled. The resource `timber/day` forecast is calculated from the exact same yield, tree-and-stump speed, queued work, staffing, weather and local conditions, so it no longer grants an unrelated continuous Logging Camp output. Holding a standing tree toggles its gold target: an unmarked tree moves to the top of the tree queue, while gold-marked priority stumps are always cleared first; holding the marked tree again removes it from that priority queue. A priority tree outside every camp circle uses the same five-hour full-crew base pace. Once felled, it becomes a gold-marked priority stump; removing the priority from a standing tree does not alter an already-created priority stump. The full order is priority stumps, priority trees, ordinary stumps, mature managed trees, then unmarked wild trees. Removing any stump recovers 1 extra timber and permanently converts that forest tile into new walkable, buildable clearing. In **After the Fire**, the same action automatically reveals all adjacent forest tiles that contain no standing tree or stump as fire-scar clearing; waterways never convert. A camp automatically pauses all tree and stump work when timber storage is full, preventing needless habitat loss and discarded materials. Its inspection panel offers a saved manual override for deliberate clearing; while that override is active, work continues but excess timber is lost. A camp also stops when it has no local wild trees, managed trees, manually prioritized tree, or remaining stump work. A nearby 4 × 4 Wood Farm provides renewable timber: the farm tracks 16 separate tree plots, every harvested plot takes five in-game days to regrow, and mature plots are harvested before unmarked wild trees.

The Founders’ Hearth stores up to 400 food, water, timber, and stone each. Every 2 × 2 Storehouse adds another 200 capacity to each resource. Dedicated 2 × 2 stores add 500 capacity only for their matching resource: Farm Barn for food, Water Tank for water, Timber Yard for timber, and Stone Depot for stone. The Farm Barn must share an edge with a Field Farm, has no workers or output, and applies ongoing soil pressure; a Field Farm cannot be demolished if doing so would leave an attached Barn without another edge-adjacent Field Farm. The 3 × 3 Large Storehouse adds 500 capacity to every resource. Excess production is discarded when that resource’s storage is full, and destroying storage can also discard anything above the new limit.

Housing, Storehouses, Village Wells, Compost Yards, Children’s Greens, Town Parks, Wild Sanctuaries, and Windmills operate without assigned workers. Rain Gardens also keep their normal automatic output without staff, but can accept up to three optional daytime helpers; three helpers raise water output and filtration to 2× while the automatic baseline continues overnight. Field Farms now accept three farmers: one produces 0.5×, two preserve the normal 1× rate, and the third raises total output, water use, and operational ecological pressure to 2×. Optional Rain Garden jobs are filled only after required village jobs.

The **Decorations** tab contains compact 1 × 1 village details and a 2 × 2 Stone Statue. Natural decorations add +0.01 to wildlife or biodiversity each day; built decorations apply −0.01 soil health/day. The Stone Statue applies −0.10 soil health/day because of its heavier foundation. Windmills have a three-tile sound-pollution radius; other loud work sites retain their five-tile buffer.

Workshops, quarries, logging camps, and other marked polluters reduce nearby farm/orchard output; farms do not pollute one another, and stone/timber producers never reduce one another’s output. A polluter beside the forest also costs 0.1 wildlife per day for each standing tree immediately surrounding its footprint.

Noise pollution is modelled separately from ecosystem damage. Logging Camps, Stone Quarries and Civic Workshops create strong daytime noise across five tiles, while automatic Windmills create a much weaker hum throughout the day and night across three tiles. Occupied homes inside a source’s noise zone reduce village morale and, at a smaller rate, health; empty housing and distant homes are not counted as resident exposure. Hovering or inspecting a loud building shows its purple noise buffer and exact full-day impact. A Logging Camp becomes quiet at night, when it runs out of work, or when full timber storage automatically pauses it.

Weather uses condition-specific, real-world-inspired durations instead of one generic timer. Thunderstorms average about two in-game hours, cloudy systems four days, heatwaves three days, steady rain twelve hours, and droughts seven days; every condition has a natural duration range. The header shows how much time remains. Visuals and production effects still fade gradually between conditions, with shorter fronts receiving faster but visible transitions. Pausing freezes the duration countdown, transition, mist, precipitation, and weather-driven tree movement. Deciduous leaves begin fading from green during summer’s final three days, continue changing through autumn’s first two days, then hold stable per-tree shades ranging from red through orange to yellow. Their canopies fade away across autumn’s last three days, remain completely leafless in winter, and fade back to green during spring’s first three days. A stable 10% of trees are evergreens and remain green all year.

Random events can now occur at any time of day, with a rolled interval between one-third of a day (at most three events per day) and seven days. Difficulty and survival time bias the roll without breaking those limits. The expanded pool includes wildfire, flooding, a multi-day village illness, pollinator decline, invasive vines, fallen-tree habitat, migration, wildlife conflicts, shortages and trade. Illness temporarily reduces health, morale and staffed output; Clinics reduce its harm.

The opening screen also includes eight prebuilt challenge villages, each with four scenario goals and its own starting crisis:

- **Winter Watch** — prepare an autumn homestead for the second spring.
- **The Dry River** — recover water stores and water quality during drought.
- **Timber Debt** — retire destructive logging and restore forest cover.
- **Smoke Valley** — separate pollution from crops and clean an industrial village.
- **After the Fire** — stabilize a burned watershed with rain gardens, compost, connected waterways and renewed forest cover. Most surviving fire-damaged trunks stay permanently black and leafless and yield 30% less timber. Clearing one of their stumps reveals adjacent treeless fire scar as clearing without erasing creek or river tiles.
- **The Poisoned River** — remove the sources of toxic runoff, then rebuild water, soil and air quality.
- **The Silent Fields** — protect pollinators and habitat after wildlife and biodiversity have collapsed.
- **The Green City** — grow a dense mini-city while raising education and habitat health.

The three restoration emergencies begin with less than 40% ecosystem health and an active crisis that lowers the overall ecosystem by a displayed 1.0 point per day. Their extra decline stops only after the scenario’s stated containment actions are completed; ordinary ecological pressures and recovery continue, so completing the repair goals still requires long-term stewardship.

In every scenario and freeplay difficulty, the settlement is lost if overall ecosystem health drops below 5%, or if any one of the six ecosystem indicators reaches 0%. This makes a weak link dangerous even when the average still looks safe.

Freeplay now has five Steward’s Path chapters. The final chapter, **A Living Legacy**, asks the village to reach Day 100, maintain a Compost Yard, Rain Garden, Wild Sanctuary and Town Park, restore every ecosystem indicator to at least 90, and reach a displayed 100% ecosystem score. The persistent **Perfect Balance** achievement is unlocked by reaching that displayed 100% score on Harsh difficulty.

The current village and all unlocked achievements save locally in the browser.
