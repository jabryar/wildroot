# Wildroot Village — three-minute presentation script

Aim for a calm speaking pace of about 120 words per minute, with natural pauses while changing slides. The timing below totals approximately three minutes.

## Slide 1 — Wildroot Village · 0:00–0:15

Hello, I’m Daniel Bryar, and I’m presenting *Wildroot Village*, created by James, who is 13. It is an environmental learning game about growing a successful village without damaging the ecosystem that supports it.

## Slide 2 — Why James created it · 0:15–0:45

James came up with this idea yesterday while thinking about climate change and global warming. He wanted to help children understand that even one simple building can change its surroundings. A building may remove or protect habitat, consume or capture water, damage or restore soil, create pollution or noise, and affect people. That is why learning is integrated directly into the game. The goal is a thriving village supported by a healthy ecosystem.

## Slide 3 — Core game loop · 0:45–1:05

The game follows four steps: plan, build, monitor and adapt. Players balance people, food, water, timber and stone while watching the environment. Short-term production can create long-term damage, and the settlement can collapse if the ecosystem becomes critically unhealthy.

## Slide 4 — Interface and controls · 1:05–1:25

The interface keeps all the important evidence visible. The left panel is for construction, the centre is the living map, and the right panel shows ecosystem health and advice. Players can pan, zoom, rotate buildings, inspect residents and pause time.

## Slide 5 — Ecosystem model · 1:25–1:50

The ecosystem has six connected indicators: forest cover, wildlife, water quality, soil health, clean air and biodiversity. The Eco Coach identifies the weakest or fastest-falling area and explains the cause. A good average cannot hide one collapsed system, so every indicator matters.

## Slide 6 — People and resources · 1:50–2:15

Residents have ages, homes, jobs and daily journeys. Staffed buildings operate during the day, while automatic services can continue overnight. Storage limits, farm staffing, forestry, pollution and noise all create trade-offs. A productive building can still be harmful if it is badly placed or overused.

## Slide 7 — Scenarios and risk · 2:15–2:40

Eight scenarios turn these systems into different environmental problems, including drought, destructive logging, industrial pollution, wildfire recovery and biodiversity loss. Weather, seasons and random events keep conditions changing, so players must respond instead of using the same solution every time.

## Slide 8 — Learning and final goal · 2:40–3:00

Learning happens through 20 field missions, 22 knowledge checks, the Eco Coach, inspections and scenarios. The final challenge is to reach Day 100 with every ecosystem indicator above 90 and an overall score of 100 percent. The message is simple: build, measure, adapt and leave enough wildness for tomorrow.
